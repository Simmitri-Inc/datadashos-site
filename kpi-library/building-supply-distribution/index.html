Why KPIs matter

Supply houses live on thin margins, big inventories and contractor credit. KPIs show which products, branches and customers actually make money, and where cash is stuck.

Example scenario

Example scenario

A three-branch roofing and lumber supplier grew sales but cash kept getting tighter. Tracking customer profitability and cost per delivery showed several large contractor accounts ordered small, frequent hot-shot deliveries that cost more than they earned. Dead stock was also tying up months of cash. They set delivery minimums, cleared slow lines and tightened credit reviews, then tracked all three weekly.

Supplier + Installer Shared KPIs

Stop paying for shortages.

When an installer is short on material, someone pays for a hot-shot delivery, often around $300, and a crew sits waiting. When suppliers and installers track the same shortage data, they can fix the cause instead of paying for it on every job.

Shared KPIWhat it tells youWhy it matters
Short-shipped job rateJobs delivered missing at least one itemThe core shortage problem
Shortages by material typeWhich products come up short mostShows what to stock, pack or check

Hot-shot cost per account or crewWhich jobs and crews trigger themTargets the fix
Order accuracyDelivered quantities match the takeoffPrevents shortages before the truck leaves
Crew wait time from shortagesHours lost waiting for materialThe installer's hidden cost
Shortage trendShortage rate over timeProves the fixes are working
  • Also track: hot-shot cost recovered vs. absorbed. How much of each month's hot-shot cost is billed back to the contractor, and how much the supplier absorbs. It shows the real margin hit, and which accounts need delivery terms.
  • Also track: cost and speed of filling a shortage. Compare each emergency delivery method (hot-shot, courier, freight) by cost per delivery and time to site, so you can see which one saves the most money and crew time.
